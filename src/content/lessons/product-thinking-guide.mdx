A person says, “Add a button that reminds me every morning to take out the rubbish.” You could build that button. But first, picture what happens at home: the rubbish is still inside when the collection truck arrives. Maybe nobody knows whose job it is. Maybe a morning reminder arrives after the useful moment. Maybe reminders already get ignored.

**You are not here to create software. You are here to solve user problems.** Today you will practice finding the problem beneath a request and choosing the smallest useful thing to try. You will not build a feature today. Success is a short, concrete explanation of whose problem this is, what happens now, what one change might help, and what you would observe if it did.

## Start with the last real occurrence

Ask one person about a recent responsibility they forgot or found hard to coordinate. You can use a household chore, a missed message, a confusing form, or a delivery that was hard to track. Ask about a particular occasion rather than what they think a future app should do.

<ul class="question-list">
<li>What were you trying to do?</li>
<li>When did it become difficult?</li>
<li>What did you do instead?</li>
<li>What was frustrating, costly, or risky?</li>
<li>How did you know the task was finished, or that it was missed?</li>
</ul>

Write down what the person actually said before interpreting it. Their suggested feature is useful evidence about their experience, but it is still an idea to test.

## Separate the request from the problem

For the rubbish example, the first useful change might be a visible owner and due time on a shared list. A morning notification can wait until you know that timing is the problem.

## Try one small intervention

Before doing anything, predict what should change. If you make ownership visible, perhaps both people can name who will take the rubbish out before collection day. If you try a reminder, perhaps the task is completed before the truck arrives. Pick a signal you can actually observe, then ask what else could explain it.

A safe first test can be simple: write the owner's name and due time on paper for one week, or show a rough screen to the person and ask what they would do next. You do not need a built app to learn whether the idea makes sense.

<ol class="mental-model-steps">
<li>Predict what the person will do with the small change.</li>
<li>Let them try it without explaining every step.</li>
<li>Record what they did and where they hesitated.</li>
<li>Try an edge case: an unassigned task or a missed due time.</li>
<li>Repeat the ordinary case to see whether it still works.</li>
<li>Say what this one test cannot prove.</li>
</ol>

<Response id="product-thinking-day-test" prompt="What is the smallest change you would test? Predict the result, name the normal check and one safe edge case, then say what evidence would show less pain and what would remain uncertain." rows={11} />

## Explain your decision

Tell a mentor or teammate the story in plain language: “This person was trying to ___. Today they ___. The hard part was ___. We tried ___. We saw ___. We still do not know ___.” If the evidence does not support your first idea, change the idea. A smaller, clearer problem is progress.
